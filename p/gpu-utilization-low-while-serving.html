Why Your GPU Sits at 30% While Serving Requests

A card at 30% is not a card you need to replace. It is a card waiting.

In short

Generation is memory-bandwidth bound, not compute bound. One request at a time leaves most of the card idle, and batching fixes that without buying hardware.

What that percentage is measuring

Generation is memory-bandwidth bound, not compute bound. One request at a time leaves most of the card idle because the arithmetic units finish long before the weights arrive from memory.

That is why a 30% reading is not a hardware problem. The card has capacity; nothing is asking it to use that capacity. Batching converts headroom into throughput — the same weight matrix serves several requests in one pass instead of being read several times.

Static batching versus continuous batching

Static batching waits for a full batch, runs it, waits again. Throughput improves, latency worsens, and the worst case is a short request stuck behind a long one for the whole batch duration.

Continuous batching admits new requests as slots free up at each decode step. That is the mechanism behind most of the throughput gains people attribute to "a faster inference server" — the scheduler is doing the work, not the kernel.

The cost is that latency becomes a distribution instead of a number. Measure the mean and batching looks free; measure p99 or you will ship a regression nobody reports until users say "sometimes it hangs".

Measure these before changing anything

  • Tokens per second under steady load — not from a single curl.
  • Time to first token, separately from total time. Different causes, different fixes.
  • p50 and p99 latency at the concurrency you actually serve.
  • Queue depth over time. Flat means you are not the bottleneck; growing means you are.

Common questions

Does batching make every request slower?

Slightly, yes — while total throughput rises a lot. That is the trade. If one user's latency is a hard requirement, cap batch by size rather than by waiting for a full batch.

Will a bigger GPU fix low utilisation?

No — it lowers utilisation further. If the card is idle because nothing is scheduled on it, a bigger card is idle more expensively.

Should I just raise max batch size?

Only together with a KV cache calculation. Batch size multiplies cache usage, and the OOM that follows arrives under load — which makes it look like a stability problem instead of a sizing one.

A measurement pass that takes an hour

  • Step 1. Load-test at the concurrency you expect, not at 1.
  • Step 2. Record GPU utilisation, tokens per second, and p99 together.
  • Step 3. If utilisation is under 50% with a flat queue, enable batching.
  • Step 4. Re-measure p99 — if it moved more than you can accept, cap the batch instead of disabling batching.
  • Step 5. Only then consider a larger card, and bring the numbers.
